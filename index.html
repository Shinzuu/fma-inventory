One screenRules and formulas

Beginning inventory + Purchases = Cost of goods available for sale
Cost of goods available for sale − Ending inventory = Cost of goods sold
Net sales − Cost of goods sold = Gross profit  ·  Gross profit ÷ Net sales = Gross profit rate

Ownership, classification, disclosure

  • FOB shipping point → buyer owns goods in transit. FOB destination → seller owns them.
  • Consigned goods stay with the consignor; the holder never counts them.
  • Merchandiser: one Inventory account. Manufacturer: raw materials, work in process, finished goods. All current assets.
  • Cost = all expenditures to acquire the goods and make them ready for sale.
  • Disclose: classifications, basis of accounting, costing method. LIFO for tax ⇒ LIFO for reporting (conformity rule).
  • Perpetual: records update at every sale. Periodic: only at period end. FIFO gives the same answer in both; LIFO and average do not.
Answer shapeunits + cost available → units left → cost them by the method → available − ending = COGS → net sales − COGS = gross profit.

Rapid fireShort answers

Define cost of goods available for sale.rules
Beginning inventory plus purchases: everything the business could have sold during the period.
What is included in the cost of inventory?rules
All expenditures necessary to acquire the goods and put them in a condition ready for sale.
FIFO in one line.rules
The costs of the earliest goods purchased are the first charged to cost of goods sold, so the newest costs stay in ending inventory.
LIFO in one line.rules
The costs of the latest goods purchased are charged to cost of goods sold first, so the oldest costs stay in ending inventory.
How is the weighted-average unit cost found?rules
Cost of goods available for sale ÷ total units available for sale. In the Houston example, $12,000 ÷ 1,000 = $12.00.
Must the cost flow assumption match the physical flow?rules
No. Only specific identification follows actual units.
Which method gives the lowest tax when prices rise, and why?rules
LIFO: the newest and dearest costs go to cost of goods sold, so net income and tax are lowest.
What is the LIFO conformity rule?rules
A company using LIFO for tax purposes must also use LIFO in its financial statements.
Who includes goods in transit?rules
Whoever holds legal title. FOB shipping point: the buyer, from the moment the carrier accepts the goods. FOB destination: the seller, until delivery.
Who counts consigned goods?rules
The consignor, who still owns them. The consignee holds them to sell for a fee but never owns them.
What must be disclosed about inventory?rules
The major classifications, the basis of accounting, and the costing method used.
Ending inventory is understated. What happens?errors
Cost of goods sold is overstated; net income, assets and stockholders' equity are understated.
Why do inventory errors affect two periods?errors
This year's ending inventory is next year's beginning inventory, so the error reverses and the two years together are correct.
Which method is identical under periodic and perpetual?6A
FIFO. LIFO and average-cost differ, because a perpetual sale can only use the layers on hand at that moment.
When is a new moving average computed?6A
After every purchase and after a purchase return, never after a sale.
In a perpetual system, how is a sales return recorded?6A
The goods go back into inventory at the cost they were charged out at, and cost of goods sold is reduced by that amount.
P6.5A: how are the returns handled?problems
The purchase return cuts units and cost available for sale (10 units, $460). The sales return cuts units sold and sales (15 units, $1,050), leaving net sales of $11,590 and 160 units sold.
P6.8A: fastest check on the three answers?problems
Cost of goods sold plus ending inventory must equal $5,460 under every method, with net sales fixed at $5,680.
